Muscle + Performance Cardio Day (~100–110 min)
Focus: Back width, shoulders, glutes and hamstrings, plus VO2max development. Lift first, then intervals, then core, then cool-down.

Training intent:
  • Upper body (back, shoulders, rear delt, arms): definition, not bulk. Moderate loads, higher reps (12–20), 3–4 reps in reserve, shorter rest (45–60 sec), full range, controlled tempo. Never grind a rep — avoid low-rep near-max loading here.
  • Lower body (glutes, hamstrings): heavier 6–12 rep strength range, unchanged. This builds the glute/hip shape.

P8: RKC Plank
What is it
A hardstyle plank variation. Looks like a normal forearm plank — instead of just holding, you create full-body tension.
How to do it
Set up on forearms, elbows under shoulders. Legs straight, feet about hip-width or a bit narrower. Tuck pelvis slightly so ribs stay down. Squeeze glutes hard. Squeeze quads. Pull elbows toward toes without actually moving. Pull toes toward elbows without actually moving. Brace abs as if someone is about to punch you.
What you should feel
Deep abdominal tension. Glutes and quads working. Sometimes lats too. Much more intense than a regular plank.
How long
Usually short holds: 10–30 seconds. Quality over duration.
Why it's in the plan
Builds waist control and trunk stiffness. Core bracing without side-bending or heavy oblique loading.
Common mistakes
Lower back sagging. Hips too high. Just hanging out instead of creating tension. Holding too long and losing position.
Quick cue
Squeeze everything and drag elbows to toes.
P9: 90/90 Breathing
What is it
A breathing drill to get ribs stacked over pelvis. Switches down from a flared-rib posture. Hips at ~90°, knees at ~90°. Usually lying on back, feet on bench/wall/chair.
How to do it
Lie on back. Feet flat on wall/bench/chair, hips and knees both ~90°. Lightly tuck pelvis so lower back is gently heavy on floor. Keep ribs down, not flared. Inhale quietly through nose. Exhale fully through mouth like fogging a mirror. At end of exhale, feel abs turn on without crunching. Pause briefly. Inhale again without losing rib/pelvis position. Cue: gently pull heels down wall without moving them.
What you should feel
Lower ribs coming down on the exhale. Abs turning on. Less arch in the low back. Sometimes hamstrings lightly if using the wall correctly.
How much
Usually 2 rounds of 3–5 breaths. Quality over rushing.
Why it's in the plan
Improves waist control. Helps core positioning for lifts. Can reduce over-arching through the lower back. Pairs well with vacuum holds, planks, and glute training.
Common mistakes
Inhaling into chest only. Arching the lower back. Forcing a huge pelvic tuck. Pushing with quads instead of lightly using hamstrings. Rushing the breaths.
Quick cue
Exhale fully, ribs down, heels lightly pull down the wall.
P10: Vacuum Holds
What are they
A core drill where you gently draw your abdomen inward and hold while breathing under control. Trains the transverse abdominis — acts like an internal corset.
How to do them
Stand tall, kneel, or lie on your back. Exhale fully. After the exhale, gently pull belly button inward and slightly up. Think "zip the waist in," not crunch down. Keep ribs stacked over pelvis. Hold the tension without shrugging or folding over. Breathe lightly if you can, or use short holds after the exhale. This is a drawing-in drill, not bracing as hard as possible.
What you should feel
Deep tension around the waist. A drawing-in feeling through the lower abdomen. Not a hard crunch or braced-out stomach.
How much
Start with 3–5 sets of 15–30 seconds. Quality over forcing longer holds.
Why it's in the plan
Helps with waist control. Improves awareness of ribcage/pelvis position. Pairs well with breathing drills, planks, and glute work. Supports the appearance of a tighter midsection.
Common mistakes
Sucking in from the chest only. Holding your breath too aggressively. Rounding forward. Gripping hip flexors or glutes too hard. Confusing it with bracing as hard as possible.
Quick cue
Exhale, ribs down, draw the lower waist inward.
